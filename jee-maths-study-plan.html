How to build a JEE maths study plan

A useful plan does not need to fill every hour. It needs to make the next study session clear and leave room to review what you have learned.

1. Map the work before scheduling it

Start with the current official syllabus for your exam. List its topics under algebra, coordinate geometry, calculus, trigonometry and other areas named in the syllabus. Mark each topic as unfamiliar, in progress or ready for revision. If a chapter depends on another, learn the prerequisite first: for example, strengthen functions before beginning limits and differentiation.

2. Give each week a small goal

Choose one main topic and one older topic to revisit. Divide the main topic into concepts, worked examples and independent questions. Make the goal specific: “understand quadratic roots and solve a mixed set” is easier to act on than “finish algebra”. Keep a short buffer for schoolwork, unexpected delays and difficult exercises.

3. Use a repeatable study block

  1. Recall: spend a few minutes writing what you remember from the last session.
  2. Learn: study a small concept and work through examples without rushing.
  3. Practise: solve questions independently, beginning with straightforward applications before moving to mixed problems.
  4. Review: record errors and decide what to revisit next time.

The length of a block can vary. A focused hour with a short break is more useful than a long session in which attention has disappeared.

4. Deal with backlog without abandoning current work

List unfinished lessons and identify which ones are prerequisites for the current topic. Reserve a regular, limited backlog slot while continuing with current lessons. Do not count a video as completed learning until you have attempted related questions. If the backlog is large, ask a teacher for help selecting the most important prerequisites.

5. Review your plan every week

At the end of the week, check whether you can solve questions without notes. Move unfinished work forward, reduce the next week's scope if necessary, and keep time for rest. Use official sources for the latest syllabus and exam rules; batch schedules and exam patterns may change.
